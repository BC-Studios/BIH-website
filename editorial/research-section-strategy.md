# Research Section Strategy

## Positioning

Use `Research` or `Knowledge`, not `Blog`.

Suggested section title:

> Research
>
> Designing Human States

Suggested description:

> A collection of essays exploring how neuroscience, architecture, movement, music, and technology shape human experience.

## Editorial Promise

Each essay should feel like part of a book that happens to be published as articles. The work should be strong enough to survive scrutiny from a neuroscientist, and useful enough that universities, studios, and coaches could cite it.

The tone is calm, curious, and precise. The confidence should come from evidence, not adjectives.

Every article supports the same central thesis:

> The next frontier in human performance is not the optimisation of the individual. It is the design of environments that help humans think, move, and connect more effectively.

## Article Architecture

Every essay follows the same architecture:

1. A paradox: the problem.
2. Why the current industry thinks about it incorrectly.
3. What neuroscience has discovered: a chronological story.
4. The leading researchers and landmark experiments.
5. What we know and what we do not know.
6. What this means for designing movement experiences.
7. The future of the field.
8. Open questions.

## Recommended Page Metadata

Each essay should expose this information near the top:

- Title
- Subtitle
- One sentence thesis
- Reading time
- Difficulty
- Researchers featured
- Key concepts
- Primary disciplines

## Premium Research Elements

Use these selectively, but consistently:

- Timeline of discovery
- Scientist cards
- Landmark experiment callouts
- Glossary terms
- Reading list
- Open questions
- Source notes with original links

## Voice

Write like documentary research, not marketing.

Prefer:

> Current evidence suggests that environmental factors influence attention, recovery, and movement.

Avoid:

> The nervous system loves spaces that feel good.

No filler. No wellness cliches. No AI tone. No emojis. No "imagine". No dramatic or inspirational language.

Prefer precise claim language:

- Current evidence suggests...
- Several independent laboratories have observed...
- The strongest evidence is...
- The evidence remains preliminary for...
- This may increase the probability of...

## Researcher-Led Storytelling

Tell the story through scientists, not only through concepts.

Every major researcher or landmark study should be introduced with:

- Name
- Field
- Year
- Question
- Experiment or method
- Finding
- Why it matters

## Sansara Concepts

Introduce original Sansara concepts as language, not marketing:

- Environmental Friction: every unnecessary sensory conflict that consumes attention.
- Temporal Architecture: the deliberate design of rhythm, pacing, and transitions over time.
- Sensory Coherence: how consistently all environmental signals support the same behavioural objective.
- State Engineering: the intentional design of environments that increase the probability of beneficial psychological and physiological states.

## Sansara Boundary

Do not over-insert Sansara into the body of the essays. The essays should earn authority by explaining the field clearly. Sansara can appear lightly in the final implication layer, but the article should not read as a sales argument.

## Required Ending

Every article ends with `Open Questions`, not a conventional conclusion.

The final section should clarify what current evidence suggests, what remains unknown, and which questions may define the next decade of movement science.
