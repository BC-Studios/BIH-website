# Sansara Research Journal Writing Rules

Treat the series like a book that happens to be published as articles.

The standard: each essay should be strong enough to survive scrutiny from a neuroscientist, and useful enough that studios, coaches, and universities could cite it.

## Central Thesis

Every article should support this overarching idea:

> The next frontier in human performance is not the optimisation of the individual. It is the design of environments that help humans think, move, and connect more effectively.

This is the intellectual identity of Sansara.

## 1. Every Statement Must Earn Its Place

No filler.

No wellness cliches.

Avoid unsupported phrases such as:

- The nervous system loves...
- Science proves...
- Your brain is wired for...
- This creates transformation...

Prefer:

- Current evidence suggests...
- Several independent laboratories have observed...
- In this study, the researchers found...
- This may increase the probability of...
- The evidence is strongest for...
- The evidence remains preliminary for...

## 2. Every Article Begins With A Paradox

Begin with a problem, not a fact.

Examples:

> We spend billions optimising athletes.
>
> Yet almost nobody optimises the rooms in which they train.

> We carefully design running shoes.
>
> We almost never design the sensory environment surrounding movement.

The paradox should create the central tension of the article.

## 3. Tell The Story Through Scientists

Do not lead with abstract concepts when a human research story is available.

Avoid:

> Predictive processing explains...

Prefer:

> In the nineteenth century Hermann von Helmholtz proposed that perception is a form of unconscious inference. More than a century later, Karl Friston transformed that philosophical idea into a mathematical framework now known as the Free Energy Principle.

Readers remember people, questions, methods, and turning points.

## 4. Every Major Researcher Gets Introduced

Use this format for major researchers and landmark studies:

```text
Roger Ulrich

Architectural psychologist

1984

Question:
Can a hospital window influence recovery?

Experiment:
224 patients recovering from gallbladder surgery.
Compared patients whose windows overlooked trees with patients facing a brick wall.

Finding:
Patients with views of nature had shorter hospital stays, required fewer strong analgesics, and received fewer negative nursing notes.

Why it matters:
The environment became part of medical treatment.
```

This makes the writing documentary rather than decorative.

## 5. Never Oversell

Every important paper should include:

- What we know
- What we do not know

This separates Sansara from wellness writing that treats early or narrow findings as settled truth.

## 6. Introduce Original Sansara Concepts As Language

These are not marketing claims. They are reusable concepts.

### Environmental Friction

Every unnecessary sensory conflict that consumes attention.

Examples:

- Buzzing lights
- Unpredictable music
- Echo
- Visual clutter
- Poor transitions

Goal: reduce environmental friction.

### Temporal Architecture

The deliberate design of rhythm, pacing, and transitions over time.

### Sensory Coherence

How consistently all environmental signals support the same behavioural objective.

### State Engineering

The intentional design of environments that increase the probability of beneficial psychological and physiological states.

Important wording: use "increase the probability", not "create".

## 7. Finish Like Nature Reviews

Do not end with a conventional conclusion.

End with:

## Open Questions

Use this pattern:

```text
Current evidence suggests that environmental factors influence attention, recovery, and movement.

Several important questions remain unanswered.

- Can adaptive lighting improve motor learning?
- Does synchronising music, movement, and light produce additive effects compared with each intervention alone?
- Can environmental coherence be objectively measured?
- Which biomarkers best predict immersion?
- How much variation should exist before novelty becomes distraction?

Answering these questions may define the next decade of movement science.
```

## 8. No AI Tone

No emojis.

No "imagine".

No dramatic storytelling.

No inspirational language.

Write as though an MIT neuroscientist who secretly loves yoga is explaining a fascinating problem over coffee.

Calm. Curious. Precise.

The confidence should come from evidence, not adjectives.
