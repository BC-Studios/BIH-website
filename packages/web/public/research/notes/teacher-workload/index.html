Discovery

University research story · 1988

In 1988, John Sweller's cognitive-load research made a hidden cost visible: learning can be harmed when working memory is spent on unnecessary complexity. In a studio, the teacher faces a related burden, holding sequence, timing, music, bodies, safety and atmosphere in mind at once.

Result: The result gives teacher workload a research spine without overclaiming. Teaching is invisible cognitive labour. Better systems should reduce sequencing and attentional load so the teacher can return to observation, presence and practice.
