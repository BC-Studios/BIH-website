Biological lighting

How does light shape readiness recovery attention and atmosphere?

Sources

  • Phototransduction by Retinal Ganglion Cells That Set the Circadian Clock

    David M. Berson; Felice A. Dunn; Motoharu Takao · 2002 · Science

    Showed that retinal ganglion cells innervating the suprachiasmatic nucleus can respond directly to light even when rod and cone input is blocked; this is a landmark source for biological lighting beyond image-forming vision.

    PubMedDOI

  • A Novel Human Opsin in the Inner Retina

    Ignacio Provencio et al. · 2000 · The Journal of Neuroscience

    Identified melanopsin expression in human and mammalian inner retina, with localization suggesting a role in non-image-forming responses such as circadian regulation and melatonin suppression.

    PubMedDOI

  • Regulation of Mammalian Circadian Behavior by Non-rod, Non-cone, Ocular Photoreceptors

    Melanie S. Freedman et al. · 1999 · Science

    Demonstrated in mice that circadian photoentrainment can persist without rods and cones but is abolished by removing the eyes; useful for the claim that ocular light biology is not reducible to visual brightness.

    PubMedDOI

  • Melanopsin (Opn4) Requirement for Normal Light-Induced Circadian Phase Shifting

    Satchidananda Panda et al. · 2002 · Science

    Showed that melanopsin contributes to normal circadian phase shifting responses to light; useful for separating strong circadian timing evidence from weaker claims about general mood or atmosphere.

    PubMedDOI

  • CIE S 026/E:2018 CIE System for Metrology of Optical Radiation for ipRGC-Influenced Responses to Light

    CIE · 2018 · International Commission on Illumination

    Defines quantities and spectral sensitivity functions for describing optical radiation that stimulates the five photoreceptor classes relevant to ipRGC-influenced responses; important for translating biology into lighting measurement.

    DOI

  • Measuring and Using Light in the Melanopsin Age

    Robert J. Lucas et al. · 2014 · Trends in Neurosciences

    Review and consensus-style guidance arguing that light should be specified in ways that account for melanopsin and other photoreceptor inputs, not only photopic lux; useful for design claims about measurement and specification.

    PubMedDOI
