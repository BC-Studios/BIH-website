Discovery

University research story · 1979

In 1979, James Gibson reframed perception from a university psychology tradition into something more alive: the world is perceived through possibilities for action. Decades later, predictive regulation and distributed cognition extended that idea across body, tools, people and environments.

Result: The result is the logic behind state engineering. A room is not a backdrop, and a practice is not only an exercise prescription. Conditions can be arranged so attention, action and regulation become easier to find.

Sources

  • Potential self-regulatory mechanisms of yoga for psychological health

    Tim Gard; Jessica J. Noggle; Crystal L. Park; David R. Vago; Angela Wilson · 2014 · Frontiers in Human Neuroscience

    Review proposing self-regulatory pathways through which yoga may affect psychological health, including attention control, emotion regulation, body awareness, and stress physiology.

    PubMedDOI

  • The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?

    Karl Friston · 2010 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review article proposing free-energy minimisation as a unifying account of perception, action, learning, attention, and salience; useful for framing humans as active prediction systems coupled to their environments.

    PubMedDOI

  • The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception

    James J. Gibson · 1979; 2014 classic edition · Psychology Press

    Classic ecological-perception source emphasizing that environments are perceived in relation to possible action; useful for translating design features into affordances for movement and attention.

    DOI

  • Allostasis: A model of predictive regulation

    Peter Sterling · 2012 · Physiology & Behavior

    Review framing allostasis as predictive regulation: the brain anticipates needs, prioritizes resources, and adjusts bodily parameters efficiently rather than defending rigid set points.

    PubMedDOI

  • Predictions not commands: active inference in the motor system

    Rick A. Adams; Stewart Shipp; Karl J. Friston · 2013 · Brain Structure and Function

    Review proposing that motor control can be understood through active inference: actions fulfil proprioceptive predictions rather than merely executing top-down commands.

    PubMedDOI

  • Distracted and confused?: Selective attention under load

    Nilli Lavie · 2005 · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

    Review of load theory showing that perceptual and cognitive load shape how well people filter distractors and maintain selective attention.

    PubMedDOI

James Gibson

Contribution: Ecological Psychology.

Key discovery: The environment is perceived through opportunities for action, or affordances, rather than as neutral objects.

Karl Friston

Contribution: Predictive Processing and Active Inference.

Key discovery: The brain continuously predicts the world and updates those predictions through action and perception.

Edwin Hutchins

Contribution: Distributed Cognition.

Key discovery: Intelligence frequently emerges across people, tools and environments rather than existing solely within individuals.

Peter Sterling

Contribution: Allostasis.

Key discovery: Physiology is organised around prediction and adaptation rather than simply maintaining equilibrium.
