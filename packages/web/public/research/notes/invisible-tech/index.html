Discovery

University research story · 2000

In 2000, university HCI researchers James Hollan, Edwin Hutchins and David Kirsh argued that cognition does not live only inside the head. It spreads across tools, representations, people and the environment, especially when a system is designed well enough to carry part of the work.

Result: The result reframes invisible technology. A good interface is not invisible because it disappears physically; it becomes less mentally expensive. In a movement environment, technology should lower friction rather than ask for more attention.

Sources

  • Distributed cognition: toward a new foundation for human-computer interaction research

    James Hollan; Edwin Hutchins; David Kirsh · 2000 · ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction

    Argues that human-computer interaction should be understood across whole environments where cognition is distributed among people, artifacts, tools, representations, and activity over time.

    DOI

  • The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception

    James J. Gibson · 1979; 2014 classic edition · Psychology Press

    Classic ecological-perception source emphasizing that environments are perceived in relation to possible action; useful for translating design features into affordances for movement and attention.

    DOI

  • Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning

    John Sweller · 1988 · Cognitive Science

    Foundational cognitive-load paper arguing that problem-solving formats can consume working-memory resources that would otherwise support learning and schema formation.

    DOI

  • Distracted and confused?: Selective attention under load

    Nilli Lavie · 2005 · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

    Review of load theory showing that perceptual and cognitive load shape how well people filter distractors and maintain selective attention.

    PubMedDOI

  • Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind

    Andy Clark · 2016 · Oxford University Press

    Major synthesis of predictive processing and embodied cognition, useful for framing perception, action, and bodily regulation as coupled prediction-error dynamics.

    DOI

Article Research Notes

Extracted from Can Technology Become Invisible?.

Don Norman

Question: Why are some technologies intuitive while others are frustrating?

Contribution: Established many of the principles of human-centred design, affordances and cognitive usability.

Key idea: Good design reduces unnecessary cognitive effort.

Steve Jobs & Apple Human Interface Guidelines

Contribution: Popularised the philosophy that technology should become an extension of intention rather than an obstacle to it.

Legacy: Many of today's interface conventions are built upon reducing interaction complexity.

Neural Efficiency Research

Question: What differentiates experts from novices?

Finding: Experts frequently perform complex tasks while recruiting fewer unnecessary cortical resources.

Limitation: Neural efficiency depends on expertise and task demands rather than representing a universal principle.
