Sources

  • How Do You Feel Now? The Anterior Insula and Human Awareness

    A. D. Craig · 2009 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review proposing the anterior insula as a key structure for subjective bodily awareness and the integration of internal feeling states.

    PubMedDOI

  • Knowing Your Own Heart: Distinguishing Interoceptive Accuracy from Interoceptive Awareness

    Sarah N. Garfinkel; Anil K. Seth; Adam B. Barrett; Keisuke Suzuki; Hugo D. Critchley · 2015 · Biological Psychology

    Distinguishes interoceptive accuracy, confidence and awareness, clarifying why body-signal perception should be measured carefully rather than treated as one simple capacity.

    PubMedDOI

  • Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self

    Anil K. Seth · 2013 · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

    Extends predictive processing to interoception and emotion, arguing that embodied self-experience depends on inference about internal bodily causes as well as external sensory causes.

    PubMedDOI

Article Research Notes

Extracted from The Predictive Brain Meets the Yoga Studio.

Hermann von Helmholtz

Question: How can the brain perceive reality from incomplete sensory information?

Contribution: Proposed unconscious inference, arguing that perception depends upon intelligent interpretation rather than direct observation.

Karl Friston

Contribution: Free Energy Principle and Active Inference.

Finding: Living systems continuously minimise uncertainty by updating predictive models of the world.

Importance: One of the most influential theoretical frameworks in contemporary neuroscience.

Andy Clark

Contribution: Predictive Processing.

Key idea: The brain continuously generates predictions that are corrected by sensory information rather than passively receiving perception.

James Gibson

Contribution: Ecological Psychology.

Connection: Prediction is always grounded in action. Perception exists to guide behaviour within environments.
