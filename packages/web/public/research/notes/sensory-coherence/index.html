Discovery

University research story · 2008

In 2008, university neuroscience returned to a deceptively simple problem: what happens when sight, sound, touch and timing arrive together? Barry Stein and Terrence Stanford reviewed how the nervous system binds signals across senses, and how timing or spatial mismatch can change the result.

Result: The result gives sensory coherence its scientific spine. The brain is not processing separate design features one by one; it is trying to assemble a coherent cause. When light, sound, breath, voice and movement compete, attention pays the cost.

Sources

  • The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?

    Karl Friston · 2010 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review article proposing free-energy minimisation as a unifying account of perception, action, learning, attention, and salience; useful for framing humans as active prediction systems coupled to their environments.

    PubMedDOI

  • Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind

    Andy Clark · 2016 · Oxford University Press

    Major synthesis of predictive processing and embodied cognition, useful for framing perception, action, and bodily regulation as coupled prediction-error dynamics.

    DOI

  • The Restorative Benefits of Nature: Toward an Integrative Framework

    Stephen Kaplan · 1995 · Journal of Environmental Psychology

    Introduces Attention Restoration Theory as a framework for how environments can help recover directed attention; strong conceptual source for soft fascination and attention fatigue, especially when discussing natural features in designed space.

    DOI

  • Multisensory integration: current issues from the perspective of the single neuron

    Barry E. Stein; Terrence R. Stanford · 2008 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review of how neurons combine inputs across senses, including spatial and temporal constraints on when multisensory signals enhance or interfere with perception and action.

    PubMedDOI

  • Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning

    John Sweller · 1988 · Cognitive Science

    Foundational cognitive-load paper arguing that problem-solving formats can consume working-memory resources that would otherwise support learning and schema formation.

    DOI

  • Distracted and confused?: Selective attention under load

    Nilli Lavie · 2005 · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

    Review of load theory showing that perceptual and cognitive load shape how well people filter distractors and maintain selective attention.

    PubMedDOI

  • How Do You Feel Now? The Anterior Insula and Human Awareness

    A. D. Craig · 2009 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review proposing the anterior insula as a key structure for subjective bodily awareness and the integration of internal feeling states.

    PubMedDOI

  • Knowing Your Own Heart: Distinguishing Interoceptive Accuracy from Interoceptive Awareness

    Sarah N. Garfinkel; Anil K. Seth; Adam B. Barrett; Keisuke Suzuki; Hugo D. Critchley · 2015 · Biological Psychology

    Distinguishes interoceptive accuracy, confidence and awareness, clarifying why body-signal perception should be measured carefully rather than treated as one simple capacity.

    PubMedDOI

Article Research Notes

Extracted from The Missing Variable in Modern Wellness: Sensory Coherence.

Roger Ulrich

Question: Can environmental views influence recovery?

Experiment: Compared post-operative patients recovering with views of trees versus brick walls.

Finding: Patients overlooking nature required fewer strong analgesics and experienced shorter hospital stays.

Stephen & Rachel Kaplan

Contribution: Attention Restoration Theory.

Finding: Natural environments restore directed attention through soft fascination rather than sustained cognitive effort.

Karl Friston

Contribution: Predictive Processing and Active Inference.

Finding: The brain predicts coherent environmental causes rather than processing isolated sensory streams.

Bud Craig

Contribution: Modern theory of interoception.

Finding: Internal bodily awareness is fundamental to consciousness and self-regulation rather than secondary to external perception.

Hugo Critchley & Sarah Garfinkel

Contribution: Interoceptive neuroscience.

Finding: Perception of internal bodily signals influences emotion, decision-making and mental health.
