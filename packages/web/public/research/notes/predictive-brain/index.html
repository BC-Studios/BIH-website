Predictive processing

What if yoga is prediction training across body space breath and social cues?

Sources

  • Treatise on Physiological Optics

    Hermann von Helmholtz · 1867; 1925 translation · Leopold Voss; Optical Society of America

    Historical anchor for unconscious inference: perception is treated as an active interpretive process rather than passive recording of sensory input.

    Source page

  • The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?

    Karl Friston · 2010 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review article proposing free-energy minimisation as a unifying account of perception, action, learning, attention, and salience; useful for framing humans as active prediction systems coupled to their environments.

    PubMedDOI

  • Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind

    Andy Clark · 2016 · Oxford University Press

    Major synthesis of predictive processing and embodied cognition, useful for framing perception, action, and bodily regulation as coupled prediction-error dynamics.

    DOI

  • Being You: A New Science of Consciousness

    Anil Seth · 2021 · Faber and Faber

    Accessible synthesis of consciousness science that popularizes perception as controlled prediction while tying conscious selfhood to bodily and interoceptive processes.

    Source page

  • Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self

    Anil K. Seth · 2013 · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

    Extends predictive processing to interoception and emotion, arguing that embodied self-experience depends on inference about internal bodily causes as well as external sensory causes.

    PubMedDOI

  • Predictions not commands: active inference in the motor system

    Rick A. Adams; Stewart Shipp; Karl J. Friston · 2013 · Brain Structure and Function

    Review proposing that motor control can be understood through active inference: actions fulfil proprioceptive predictions rather than merely executing top-down commands.

    PubMedDOI
