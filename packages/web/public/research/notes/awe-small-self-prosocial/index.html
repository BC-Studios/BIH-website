Article Research Notes

Extracted from The Neuroscience of Awe.

Dacher Keltner

Question: What is awe, and why does it appear across cultures?

Contribution: Developed the modern psychological science of awe.

Finding: Awe consistently involves perceived vastness combined with the need to update existing mental models ("accommodation").

Limitation: Most studies rely on subjective reports and behavioural measures; the precise neural mechanisms remain an active area of research.

Psychological Studies of Awe

Finding: Experiences of awe are associated with increased prosocial behaviour, curiosity, feelings of connectedness and reduced self-focus.

Important caveat: These findings describe statistical tendencies, not universal effects. Individual experiences vary.

Environmental Psychology

Connection: Research by Roger Ulrich and the Kaplans suggests that natural environments support restoration and attention. Awe research extends this by examining experiences of profound perceptual and cognitive expansion rather than restoration alone.
