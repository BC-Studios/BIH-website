# When Teaching Replaces Practice

## The quiet paradox of becoming a great teacher.

Most people imagine yoga teachers spend their days practising yoga.

The reality is often very different.

A class begins long before students enter the room.

The teacher searches for music.

Builds a playlist.

Adjusts timings.

Plans a sequence.

Checks the class level.

Answers messages.

Prepares lighting.

Finds alternatives for injuries.

Arrives early.

Stays afterwards.

Speaks with students.

Travels across the city.

Repeats the process again.

And somewhere inside that schedule, they hope to find time to practise.

The strange consequence of becoming a successful teacher is that teaching gradually replaces practice.

The very thing that inspired someone to teach begins receiving less attention.

---

## The Invisible Work

Students experience sixty minutes.

Teachers experience many more.

Most of the work remains invisible.

Sequencing.

Music selection.

Timing transitions.

Balancing challenge and accessibility.

Managing energy.

Remembering names.

Responding to the room.

None of these tasks appear particularly demanding in isolation.

Together they become a substantial cognitive load.

Ironically, much of this work occurs before anyone steps onto a mat.

---

## The Attention Economy Has Reached Yoga

Modern teachers do not simply teach.

They also create content.

Edit videos.

Respond to messages.

Maintain social media.

Design workshops.

Build websites.

Manage bookings.

Answer emails.

The profession increasingly rewards visibility alongside teaching.

Attention has become another resource to manage.

Every administrative task consumes a small amount of cognitive energy.

None appears significant.

Together they quietly compete with practice itself.

---

## Expertise Should Reduce Friction

One of the recurring themes in neuroscience is that expertise reduces unnecessary cognitive effort.

Experienced musicians no longer consciously calculate every note.

Elite athletes no longer analyse every movement.

Flow research suggests that mastery allows executive control to become less intrusive.

Teaching should evolve similarly.

As experience grows, the mechanics of preparing a class ought to become easier.

Yet many teachers experience the opposite.

The administrative burden increases faster than the practice deepens.

The result is a subtle form of professional drift.

Teachers become managers of experiences rather than participants in them.

---

## The Real Opportunity

Much of the current conversation around artificial intelligence focuses on replacement.

Will AI replace teachers?

Will it generate sequences?

Will it write classes?

Will it create music?

These are the wrong questions.

Teaching is not primarily the production of content.

It is the cultivation of attention.

No algorithm can feel the atmosphere in a room.

Notice hesitation in a student's movement.

Recognise when silence is more valuable than another instruction.

Adapt to the emotional tone of a group.

These remain profoundly human capacities.

The opportunity is not replacing teachers.

It is protecting those capacities.

---

## Reducing The Wrong Kind Of Work

There is work that develops teaching.

And work that merely consumes time.

Searching through hundreds of songs.

Adjusting playlists.

Rebuilding sequences from scratch.

Remembering previous class structures.

Formatting notes.

Repeating administrative tasks.

These activities are necessary.

They are not where wisdom develops.

Technology is most valuable when it removes work that contributes little to practice while preserving work that contributes deeply to teaching.

---

## Returning Time To Practice

Imagine beginning preparation with inspiration rather than administration.

A teacher describes the intention for tomorrow's class.

Grounding.

Rotation.

Recovery after running.

Confidence before competition.

The system suggests possible movement pathways.

Relevant music.

Transitions.

Alternative options.

Nothing is generated automatically.

Nothing is imposed.

The teacher remains the author.

Technology becomes a collaborator.

The objective is not faster classes.

The objective is more time spent practising, observing and refining the craft itself.

---

## The Teacher Remains The Instrument

Throughout this collection we have argued that environments influence experience.

The same principle applies to teaching.

A great teacher is not defined by a sequence.

Nor by a playlist.

Nor by technology.

Those are instruments.

The teacher remains the instrument through which the experience ultimately passes.

Technology should therefore behave like a well-designed musical instrument.

Reliable.

Responsive.

Almost invisible.

It expands expression without competing for attention.

The student remembers the class.

Not the software.

---

## A Different Relationship With Technology

Perhaps the future of teaching is not automation.

It is amplification.

Technology remembers.

The teacher notices.

Technology organises.

The teacher improvises.

Technology suggests.

The teacher decides.

Technology reduces friction.

The teacher creates meaning.

The partnership becomes asymmetrical.

Exactly as it should.

---

## Beyond Productivity

The greatest gift technology can offer teachers is not efficiency.

It is presence.

An extra hour not spent searching for music becomes an hour available for practice.

A sequence remembered automatically becomes mental space available for creativity.

Reduced preparation becomes deeper observation.

Less administration becomes more teaching.

Ultimately, the purpose of technology is not to produce more classes.

It is to help teachers remain practitioners.

Because the best classes rarely emerge from software.

They emerge from teachers who continue to deepen their own relationship with practice.

---

## Open Questions

* Which parts of teaching genuinely benefit from automation?

* Which parts should remain entirely human?

* Can technology reduce cognitive load without reducing creativity?

* How should AI support teachers without becoming the author?

* What happens when teachers recover the time to become students again?

Perhaps the future of movement education will not be defined by artificial intelligence replacing teachers.

It may be defined by intelligent tools quietly giving teachers back the one resource that matters most.

**Their own practice.**
