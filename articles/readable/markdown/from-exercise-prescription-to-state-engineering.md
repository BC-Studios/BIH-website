# From Exercise Prescription to State Engineering

## Why the next revolution in human performance will not optimise people. It will optimise interactions.

Scientific revolutions rarely begin with new technology.

They begin with a new question.

For much of human history, medicine asked:

**Which organ is failing?**

As physiology matured, the question changed:

**How does the body maintain itself?**

Exercise science asked:

**How can this body move better?**

Elite sport asked:

**How can this athlete perform better?**

The quantified-self movement asked:

**How can we measure this biology more precisely?**

Each question transformed human health.

Each also shared a hidden assumption.

The unit of optimisation was always the individual.

The body.

The athlete.

The patient.

The user.

Modern neuroscience may be pointing toward a different unit altogether.

Not the individual.

The interaction between the individual and the environment.

---

## We Have Been Optimising Half of the System

Every heartbeat occurs somewhere.

Every breath occurs somewhere.

Every thought occurs somewhere.

Every movement occurs somewhere.

Yet performance science has traditionally focused on only one side of the equation.

We optimise muscles.

Nutrition.

Recovery.

Sleep.

Hormones.

Biomechanics.

Meanwhile the environment remains remarkably primitive.

Lighting designed for visibility rather than biology.

Music selected for preference rather than timing.

Architecture designed for capacity rather than cognition.

Technology competing for attention rather than protecting it.

The athlete evolved.

The environment largely did not.

This is not because the environment is unimportant.

It is because we lacked a scientific framework for understanding it.

That framework is now beginning to emerge.

---

## Evolution Never Separated The Brain From The World

Human beings did not evolve inside laboratories.

Or offices.

Or gyms.

They evolved inside environments.

Forests.

Grasslands.

Rivers.

Firelight.

Changing weather.

Social groups.

The nervous system was shaped not only by the body it controlled, but by the world with which that body continuously interacted.

From an evolutionary perspective, separating the brain from the environment is almost artificial.

The two have always formed a single adaptive system.

Perhaps this is why so many independent scientific disciplines are beginning to converge.

---

## The Quiet Revolution Across Neuroscience

Environmental psychologists demonstrated that architecture influences recovery and attention.

Circadian biologists showed that light regulates biological timing.

Motor neuroscientists discovered that rhythm organises movement.

Predictive neuroscientists proposed that perception itself depends on expectations rather than passive observation.

Ecological psychologists argued that perception exists for action.

Researchers studying distributed cognition demonstrated that intelligence often extends across people, tools and environments rather than residing within isolated brains.

None of these discoveries were intended to create a unified theory.

Yet together they point toward the same conclusion.

Human behaviour is relational.

It emerges through continuous interaction.

---

## James Gibson Changed The Question

For decades psychology described perception as a sequence.

First we perceive.

Then we act.

Professor James Gibson proposed something radically different.

The environment is not simply observed.

It offers possibilities for action.

A chair is not merely recognised as an object.

It is immediately perceived as something that can be sat upon.

A staircase invites climbing.

A doorway invites passage.

A yoga mat invites movement.

Perception is already behavioural.

The environment contains information about what actions are possible.

Gibson called these possibilities **affordances**.

The idea quietly transformed psychology.

Perhaps movement has never been something imposed upon the environment.

Perhaps movement has always been a conversation with it.

---

## Prediction Completes The Picture

Half a century later, Professor Karl Friston proposed that the brain continuously predicts rather than passively reacts.

The nervous system constantly generates models of the world.

Incoming sensory information updates those models.

Movement therefore becomes more than muscular activity.

It becomes an experiment.

Every step tests a prediction.

Every breath updates a model.

Every balance posture refines expectations about gravity.

Prediction does not stop at the edge of the skin.

It includes the environment.

The body and the world become partners in a continuous exchange of information.

---

## Intelligence Is Distributed

The same idea appears elsewhere.

Anthropologist Edwin Hutchins studied navigation aboard naval vessels.

He noticed something unexpected.

No individual understood the entire navigation process.

Intelligence emerged from interactions between sailors, instruments, maps, procedures and the ship itself.

The system was intelligent.

Not merely the people inside it.

The principle appears repeatedly.

An orchestra.

An operating theatre.

A Formula One pit crew.

A rowing team.

A movement class.

Performance emerges through relationships.

Not isolated components.

---

## The Wrong Unit Of Optimisation

Perhaps the greatest limitation of modern performance science is not the measurements themselves.

It is the unit being measured.

Heart rate.

Power output.

VO₂ max.

Strength.

Sleep.

Recovery.

These remain essential.

They describe the individual.

The next frontier may measure something different.

The quality of interaction.

Interaction between movement and rhythm.

Between breathing and attention.

Between architecture and behaviour.

Between light and biological timing.

Between people moving together.

Interaction becomes the fundamental unit.

Not because individuals disappear.

Because individuals never function independently of the environments they inhabit.

---

## From Exercise Prescription To State Engineering

Medicine prescribes pharmaceuticals.

Exercise science prescribes movement.

State Engineering proposes something different.

Not another intervention.

A different design philosophy.

We define **State Engineering** as:

> **The intentional design of interactions between people and their environments to increase the probability of beneficial cognitive, emotional and physiological states.**

Notice what this definition does not claim.

It does not claim environments create flow.

Or happiness.

Or wellbeing.

Human experience remains deeply personal.

State Engineering simply recognises that environments influence the interactions through which those states emerge.

Design therefore becomes an active component of health.

---

## A New Scientific Discipline

Architecture studies buildings.

Psychology studies behaviour.

Neuroscience studies the brain.

Exercise science studies movement.

Design studies interaction.

Perhaps the next discipline will not replace any of these.

It will connect them.

Movement studios may become the first laboratories.

Not because yoga is unique.

But because few environments change attention, breathing, movement, emotion and social interaction simultaneously.

The lessons learned there may eventually influence classrooms, hospitals, offices, museums, airports and cities.

The movement studio is simply where the future begins.

## Research Notes

### James Gibson

**Contribution:** Ecological Psychology.

**Key discovery:** The environment is perceived through opportunities for action, or affordances, rather than as neutral objects.

---

### Karl Friston

**Contribution:** Predictive Processing and Active Inference.

**Key discovery:** The brain continuously predicts the world and updates those predictions through action and perception.

---

### Edwin Hutchins

**Contribution:** Distributed Cognition.

**Key discovery:** Intelligence frequently emerges across people, tools and environments rather than existing solely within individuals.

---

### Peter Sterling

**Contribution:** Allostasis.

**Key discovery:** Physiology is organised around prediction and adaptation rather than simply maintaining equilibrium.

---

## Open Questions

* Can interactions become the fundamental unit of performance science?

* Which environmental interactions most strongly influence learning and adaptation?

* Can architecture be designed around affordances rather than aesthetics alone?

* How should we measure the quality of interaction between people and environments?

* Could State Engineering become a recognised scientific discipline?

The twentieth century taught us how to optimise the individual.

The next century may ask a different question.

**What if the true unit of human performance was never the individual at all, but the relationship between people and the worlds they inhabit?**
