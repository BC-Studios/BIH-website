# Website And Blog Series Experience

This plan combines the research-journal structure developed in this workspace with the visual and marketing guidance from `PLAN.md`.

## Purpose

The public experience should not feel like a normal blog archive. It should feel like a cinematic research journal that also works as a publishable blog series.

The website is the canonical home:

- It holds the full series structure.
- It shows cross-links, tags, visual artifacts, source notes and research base connections.
- It lets readers move linearly, conceptually or visually.

The blog/newsletter experience is the distribution layer:

- It publishes one article at a time.
- It gives each piece a strong standalone opening.
- It points readers back to the website hub for the full system.

## Experience Thesis

Sansara should read as:

> Cinematic editorial software for immersive yoga.

The research section should make the same promise in editorial form:

> Design the whole room, not just the playlist.

Each article should translate research into a usable design idea: breath timing, music structure, light state, sensory coherence, flow, teaching, measurement or studio systems.

## Audience

Primary readers:

- Yoga teachers.
- Studio owners.
- Music-led facilitators.
- Serious practitioners.
- Wellness, creative and product technologists.
- Investors or partners trying to understand the intellectual foundation of Sansara.

### Layer 3: Research Base

The research base should sit behind the articles as evidence infrastructure:

- `timeline.csv`: research lineage and historical moments.
- `research-notes.csv`: concept notes and source clusters.
- `cinematic-experiments.csv`: possible visual/interactive modules.
- `source-candidates.csv` and `references.csv`: citation work.

On the website, this can appear as:

- Research lineage modules.
- Scientist/source cards.
- Evidence blocks.
- Timeline inserts.
- Visual experiment modules.

On the blog, keep the base lighter:

- Include visible citations.
- Link back to the website for the full source library.
- Avoid overloading the newsletter with source tables.

## Article Page Pattern

Each article should feel like editorial software, not a static blog post.

Required page elements:

- Full-bleed or cinematic hero image / diagram / visual artifact.
- Article title.
- Short thesis block.
- Series position.
- Tags.
- Reading time.
- Source/evidence tags.
- Main article column.
- Desktop context rail with key ideas, related essays and research notes.
- Mobile inline related sections.
- Related Reading section.
- Open Questions.
- Source or research-note link.

Do not hide the article content behind decorative layout. The page should feel cinematic, but still read beautifully.

## Blog / Newsletter Pattern

Each published blog issue should be self-contained, but point back to the website.

Recommended structure:

1. Title.
2. One-sentence thesis.
3. Opening paradox.
4. Main essay.
5. One visual artifact or simple diagram.
6. Three key takeaways.
7. Sources / further reading.
8. Link back to:
   - full series hub,
   - previous article,
   - next article,
   - relevant tag page.

Blog articles can be shorter and more intimate than website articles, but they should not lose the research credibility.

## Combined Publishing Flow

For each article:

1. Prepare Markdown draft in `articles/readable/markdown/`.
2. Confirm title, subtitle, tags and series position.
3. Add source links and Open Questions.
4. Add or brief the visual artifact.
5. Publish the canonical website article.
6. Adapt a shorter issue only when the essay needs a distribution version.
7. Link every public issue back to the canonical page.
8. Update the series hub and tag pages.

## Visual Design Guidance

Use the `PLAN.md` direction as the website design layer:

- Dark cinematic canvas.
- Warm readable editorial surfaces.
- Teal, gold, rose and violet accents as signal colors.
- Editorial serif/display for research titles.
- Clean UI sans for navigation, tags, evidence and diagrams.
- Body text around `18-20px` with comfortable line height.
- No generic wellness imagery.
- No vague AI futurism.
- No beige wellness sameness.
- No purple-gradient SaaS look.

The research pages should feel premium and technically serious, but never medicalised.

## Trust And Accessibility

Every research page should:

- Keep citations visible for neuroscience, health-adjacent or measurement claims.
- Avoid implying medical outcomes unless substantiated.
- Meet WCAG contrast.
- Avoid color-only meaning in diagrams.
- Include captions/transcripts for audio/video.
- Make diagrams understandable without animation.
- Use plain language even when the ideas are sophisticated.

## Acceptance Criteria

The combined website/blog experience is working when:

- A visitor understands what Sansara is within five seconds.
- A teacher sees within one scroll how Sansara changes session design.
- A reader can move through the research linearly or by tag.
- Every article has a visual artifact.
- Every article links to related research and adjacent essays.
- Blog posts feel publishable on their own but clearly belong to the larger research journal.
- The research feels credible, not decorative.
- The design feels cinematic, embodied and technically serious.
