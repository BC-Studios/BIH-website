Sources

  • The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?

    Karl Friston · 2010 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review article proposing free-energy minimisation as a unifying account of perception, action, learning, attention, and salience; useful for framing humans as active prediction systems coupled to their environments.

    PubMedDOI

  • Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind

    Andy Clark · 2016 · Oxford University Press

    Major synthesis of predictive processing and embodied cognition, useful for framing perception, action, and bodily regulation as coupled prediction-error dynamics.

    DOI

  • The Restorative Benefits of Nature: Toward an Integrative Framework

    Stephen Kaplan · 1995 · Journal of Environmental Psychology

    Introduces Attention Restoration Theory as a framework for how environments can help recover directed attention; strong conceptual source for soft fascination and attention fatigue, especially when discussing natural features in designed space.

    DOI

  • Multisensory integration: current issues from the perspective of the single neuron

    Barry E. Stein; Terrence R. Stanford · 2008 · Nature Reviews Neuroscience

    Review of how neurons combine inputs across senses, including spatial and temporal constraints on when multisensory signals enhance or interfere with perception and action.

    PubMedDOI

  • Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning

    John Sweller · 1988 · Cognitive Science

    Foundational cognitive-load paper arguing that problem-solving formats can consume working-memory resources that would otherwise support learning and schema formation.

    DOI

  • Distracted and confused?: Selective attention under load

    Nilli Lavie · 2005 · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

    Review of load theory showing that perceptual and cognitive load shape how well people filter distractors and maintain selective attention.

    PubMedDOI
