## 1. The Provocative Question

Start with a paradox, not a fact.

Examples:

- We spend billions optimising athletes. Yet almost nobody optimises the rooms in which they train.
- We carefully design running shoes. We almost never design the sensory environment surrounding movement.

The reader should feel the problem before they meet the research.

## 2. What the Industry Gets Wrong

What does the current wellness, fitness, architecture, technology, or movement industry usually assume?

Be specific. Name the mistaken model.

Examples:

- Treating attention as willpower.
- Treating stress as only a mental state.
- Treating movement as mechanical output.
- Treating environment as decoration.

## 3. What Neuroscience Discovered

Tell the discovery story chronologically.

Use dates, places, researchers, and turning points. Do not summarize the field all at once. Let knowledge evolve in front of the reader.

Tell the story through scientists, not only through concepts.

## 5. What We Know And What We Do Not Know

Separate evidence from interpretation.

For each major claim, clarify:

- What current evidence suggests.
- Where the evidence is strong.
- Where the evidence remains preliminary.
- What the study does not prove.

Use this section to create intellectual honesty. Strong research essays do not pretend the field is complete.

## 6. What This Means for Designing Movement Experiences

Translate the research into design implications.

Focus on movement experiences, environments, sequencing, instruction, music, rhythm, attention, recovery, and state change.

Use Sansara concepts where they genuinely clarify the argument:

- Environmental Friction
- Temporal Architecture
- Sensory Coherence
- State Engineering

Important wording: environments can increase the probability of beneficial states. Do not claim that they create states.
